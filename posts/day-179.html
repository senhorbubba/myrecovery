Week 26 May 14, 2026

Day 179: Closer to the Other Side

"A day that balanced hard training with meaningful social connection. Physically, the rehab continues progressing into more dynamic territory, even if some movements still feel uncomfortable. Emotionally, talking to someone who already went through the same process reinforced how far I've already come."

Pain Level 1-2/10
Swelling 1/10

I woke up early and went to the gym with the plan of doing both legs and my usual back and biceps session. But once again, time became the problem. It's getting harder to fit everything into a single workout now that the sessions are becoming longer and more complex.

I decided to leave legs for later anyway because they were still tired after Tuesday's work.

Since I was working from home today, I knew I would probably find some extra time later in the afternoon to come back.

And I did.

Around 16:00, I returned to the gym and focused fully on legs. Mostly machines, but also some jumping exercises and lunges. Jumping still feels strange. Not unstable exactly, but unnatural, and I can still feel discomfort in the patellas during impact. It's one of those reminders that even though strength is coming back, the body is still adapting to more explosive movements.

Back home, I finished work and spent some time with the kids before heading out in the evening for a happy hour with a friend I hadn't seen in a while.

What made it different is that he went through a very similar surgery a few years ago.

It felt good talking to someone who genuinely understands the process without needing explanations. The frustrations, the doubts, the slow milestones, the mental side of it all.

The biggest difference though was realizing how much more positive my own tone sounded compared to earlier months.

I truly feel closer to the end now than to the beginning.

Every day is getting a little better.

I probably drank a bit too much again, but honestly, I also realized how much I needed that social recharge. Sometimes conversations and laughter recover a different part of you.

Key Takeaways

  • Dynamic movements still expose the remaining gaps in recovery
  • Training volume and intensity continue to increase steadily
  • Talking to people who experienced similar struggles creates perspective
  • The emotional tone around recovery is becoming more optimistic
  • Social connection helps restore mental energy and motivation
