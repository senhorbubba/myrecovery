Day 104: Active Recovery, Subtle Doubts

"A productive and physically active day, despite a mild hangover. Movement was present from morning to evening, mostly around the kids and structured training. Energy returned gradually. The knee handled the load well, but walking still feels slightly unnatural — a quiet reminder that recovery isn’t fully complete. A healthy day overall, with discipline slowly reasserting itself."

Pain Level 1/10
Swelling 2/10

Woke up with a slight hangover. Nothing dramatic, but enough to feel it. Quick breakfast and off to take my middle one to football practice.

While watching him play, I decided not to waste the time. PowerDot, squats, stretches, quad activation, glutes - all done on the side of the field while my little one chased the ball around. There’s something satisfying about training quietly while your kids do their thing.

Back home briefly, then he went for another practice in the building and I went to the gym. Chest and triceps today. Finished with abs. No cardio. I wanted to swim, but the pool was closed. Sometimes you plan - sometimes reality adjusts it for you.

Afternoon was full again. Games with the kids, then back to the club to watch my oldest play football this time. Long day around sports fields.

Physically I feel good. Stronger. More stable.

But walking still feels strange. Slight limp sensation. Not pain - just not fully natural. That bothers me more than discomfort would. I need to talk to my doctor. At this stage, shouldn’t walking be fully automatic again?

Lunch at the club. Trying to avoid gluten again. Slowly coming back to my diet. Slowly. No extremes, just correction.

Back home. Chill. Quick nap.

Thought about going out but decided against it. Stayed in. Watched a movie. Prioritized rest.

It was a healthy day.

Not spectacular. Not heroic.

Just consistent.

Key Takeaways

  • Small excess (alcohol) is felt the next day — even if minor.
  • Training can happen anywhere if intention is there.
  • Strength is returning, but mechanics still need attention.
  • Recovery isn’t just pain-free — it’s movement quality.
  • Discipline after imperfection matters more than perfection.
  • Rest is sometimes the smartest decision.
