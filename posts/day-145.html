Day 145: Aware but Improving

"A day that started with tennis and reflection, moved into focused recovery work, and ended with a long social night. The knee felt more relaxed after the session with Taka, even though awareness during walking is still present. Progress continues, but the body is clearly carrying the load of the week."

Pain Level 2/10
Swelling 1/10

I woke up slightly hungover and a bit earlier than usual to watch the Monte Carlo quarterfinal, Fonseca against Zverev. I woke up my middle one as well, since he had said he wanted to watch it with me. It’s funny because I remember being his age and having zero interest in watching tennis. He is the opposite. He even brought a small book about Nadal, remembering that Monte Carlo was one of his first big tournament wins. Moments like that make you realize how differently things can evolve across generations.

Fonseca eventually lost, but he fought hard. It’s great to see Brazil having a new talent coming through. There is something exciting about watching the beginning of someone’s journey.

Around midday, I went to see Taka. I explained to him something I’ve been noticing more clearly, that I am still very aware of my leg while walking. The muscles seem to contract with every step, not fully relaxed like the other side. It’s improving, but it’s still there.

The session focused on mobility, stretching, and flexion, followed by a deep tissue massage of the entire leg. It was quite intense. The muscles were clearly tight after the week of pushing harder in training. By the end, the leg felt much better. Walking became easier, less stiff, more natural again.

He advised me not to train today and instead give the body time, pushing sessions into the weekend. It made sense.

I went back home and worked for a bit, keeping the day relatively light before the evening.

At night, we had a wedding. A long one. Standing, dancing, drinking, eating, moving constantly. The leg held up well, which is a positive sign, but I can already feel that the rest of the body will pay the price tomorrow.

Another day where progress is there, but the balance between pushing and recovering remains key.

Key Takeaways

  • Walking awareness is still present but gradually improving
  • Muscle tension reflects accumulated workload
  • Targeted recovery sessions can quickly change how the body feels
  • Progress is visible even in small changes like walking ease
  • The body can handle more, but recovery still needs to be respected
