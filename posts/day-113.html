Day 113: A Quiet Monday

"Monday carried its usual resistance, with everyone moving a little slower into the week, but physically the day felt reassuring. The knee responded well, walking felt more natural, and there was a noticeable sense of strength in the quadriceps. It was not a day of big milestones, just one of those ordinary days where progress quietly shows itself."

Pain Level 1/10
Swelling 2/10

Monday mornings always seem a bit difficult, not only for me but for everyone in the house. There is always that normal struggle of getting the kids ready, keeping the pace moving, and making sure everyone leaves on time. After the usual morning routine, I got in the car and drove to the office.

What stood out today was how good the knee felt. Walking felt natural, without those false sensations that still sometimes appear, and there was a clear feeling of strength coming from the quadriceps. It made me think that maybe the recent running sessions are already helping, not only physically but also in the way they rebuild confidence during simple movement. There is something about running that seems to reconnect the body with a more normal walking pattern afterward.

At midday I had a quick lunch with my wife, then went back to work. The day itself passed without much intensity, but by the time I came home I felt tired and without much desire to do anything demanding.

The evening stayed simple. I watched some tennis from Indian Wells, played a bit with the kids, and later went downstairs for the feira in the building to eat something and buy some fruit. These ordinary little moments felt enough for the day.

Back upstairs, more tennis on TV, then a quick PowerDot massage followed by ice before bed.

Nothing exceptional today, but also nothing heavy. Just one of those days where recovery quietly continues in the background while life carries on.

Key Takeaways

  • Walking confidence improves when strength is felt in the quads
  • Running may already be influencing everyday movement
  • Ordinary days often reveal progress more honestly than intense ones
  • Not every day needs to produce something new
  • Simple routines still support recovery
