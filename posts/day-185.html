Day 185: The Light at the End of the Tunnel

"A day dominated by anticipation and finished with reassurance. The long-awaited appointment with Dr. Sergio brought encouraging news, confirmation that recovery is progressing well, and perhaps most importantly, a realistic timeline for returning to sports. For the first time, the finish line feels close enough to touch."

Pain Level 1/10
Swelling 1/10

I worked from home today, with a few meetings around the city, but honestly my mind was somewhere else most of the day.

My consultation with Dr. Sergio was in the evening.

As always, I prepared my list of questions in advance. Every appointment feels important because it gives me a chance to compare what I feel with what is actually happening inside the knee.

The meeting went really well.

Dr. Sergio was very happy with the progress. He examined the knee, checked the stability, listened to my concerns, and overall seemed pleased with where things are. There is still work to do, especially because the legs are not fully symmetrical yet, but the stability itself looks very good.

He wants me to do an MRI and come back next month. At the next visit, he also wants to perform a proper strength test to objectively measure the remaining difference between both legs.

Of course, I asked the question that has been on my mind for months.

When can I come back to sports?

His answer surprised me.

The timeline he gave me was earlier than I had expected. On one hand, it felt amazing to hear. On the other hand, it also felt a little dangerous. Sometimes getting permission is harder than being restricted because now the responsibility shifts back to me.

I know myself well enough to know that I will play it safe.

The gym comes first. Strength comes first. The return to sport needs to be earned, not rushed.

We also talked about the strange noises in the knee. Apparently, they may stay with me for a long time, possibly forever. The small aches and random pains that appear from time to time may also remain.

But functionally?

The knee should come back.

That was the most important message of the evening.

I left the consultation feeling genuinely happy.

For months, the goal felt distant and abstract.

Now it feels real.

Two more months.

Let's go.

Key Takeaways

  • Objective medical feedback reinforces confidence in recovery
  • Stability is progressing faster than strength symmetry
  • Returning to sport is becoming a question of preparation, not permission
  • Some noises and discomforts may remain without limiting function
  • The finish line finally feels close enough to see clearly
