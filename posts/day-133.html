Day 133: End of a Long Stretch

"A slow Sunday marked by fatigue, reflection, and the feeling of a week that pushed beyond balance. The body rested, but the mind started to question the lack of structure, especially around physio. It was a needed pause, but also a moment to reset priorities."

Pain Level 2/10
Swelling 2/10

Sunday started with a hangover. I slept in late with the curtains closed, shutting out the day for a while longer than usual. Even with the longer sleep, I could feel that my body was not fully recovered. The vitals were off, energy low, and everything felt slightly heavier than it should.

The morning stayed slow. Breakfast with my wife, a bit of music, no rush, no pressure. It was one of those mornings where doing less feels like the only right option.

Later we went for lunch at my parents-in-law’s place, meeting the kids and nephews. The atmosphere was easy, with time around the pool, simple conversations, food, and, again, some beer. It felt like the weekend had not really ended yet, just continuing at a slower pace.

In the evening we went to the theater to watch stand-up. A good change of rhythm, sitting, laughing, switching the mind off. After that, back home for dinner, a movie, a bit of reading, and finally ice and rest.

The day itself was calm, but it left me thinking.

This week was a stretch.

I started to feel that I didn’t do enough physio. The routine slipped, replaced by travel, social events, and constant movement without structure. My leg feels stiffer, and I can sense that I haven’t given it the consistency it needs.

That realization stayed with me at the end of the day.

Not frustration, but awareness.

I promised myself to get back to the routine next week.

Key Takeaways

  • Long weeks without structure slowly affect the body
  • Rest days help, but they do not replace consistent work
  • Awareness of slipping habits is the first step to correcting them
  • Fatigue and stiffness often come together when routine breaks
  • Resetting the routine is part of the process
