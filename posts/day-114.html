Day 114: Closer to Return

"A full day moving between training, work, family, and small reflections about how life keeps accelerating. The body handled the morning well, but by evening some old discomfort returned, reminding me that recovery still moves in waves. Emotionally, tennis no longer feels distant, which may be one of the clearest signs that mentally I am already approaching return."

Pain Level 2/10
Swelling 2/10

The day started early with a gym session focused on legs and back, followed by a quick fifteen-minute massage. Training early always gives the impression that the day begins with something already achieved, even if everything after that still needs to fit into the usual rhythm. Since I was working from home today, the transition into work felt easier, without the interruption of traffic or the pressure of getting across the city.

At lunchtime I met a friend at the club. The conversation naturally went into familiar territory: injuries, physiotherapy, recovery, and the long process of getting back to sport and normal life. These conversations seem to happen often now, maybe because once you go through something physical like this, you start noticing how many people carry their own versions of recovery. After lunch we passed by a tennis store to visit another friend, and that turned into one of those unexpectedly good moments, listening to tennis stories, catching up, and staying around that environment again.

What I noticed is that talking about tennis now feels easier. In the beginning, it carried frustration because it felt too far away. Now it feels different. I feel closer to being back, and that changes everything. The distance no longer feels abstract. It feels measurable, and that makes the desire to return even stronger.

Back home, work again, trying to finish as much as possible before the kids came back. Then the house changed completely, as it always does: chaos, screams, laughter, little arguments, treats, hugs, movement everywhere. Sometimes I genuinely wonder if every house feels this intense or if ours carries a special level of noise and life.

At the end of the day I took my middle one to the doctor. Everything is good, he is growing fast, which somehow always surprises me even when I see it every day. It also made me think how often the middle child quietly receives less focus, with so much energy naturally going to the first one because of first-time worries and to the youngest because of constant attention. Spending that time alone with him felt important. These simple doctor visits also remind you how fast time moves and how quickly children change while you are busy living inside the routine.

Back home, a great dinner, then tennis again on TV. Physically, though, the leg began to feel strange, bringing back a sensation I had a few weeks ago: pain on the outside radiating toward the hip, with a feeling of reduced mobility. Nothing dramatic, but enough to notice and remember that recovery still shifts from day to day.

Once the kids were finally asleep, I finished the day with PowerDot, ice, and the Sinner-Fonseca match. It was impressive to watch how much power an eighteen-year-old can produce. The match stayed close, and even in defeat it feels obvious that there is a real future there for the young Brazilian.

Key Takeaways

  • Talking about tennis feels different when return begins to feel real
  • Recovery still fluctuates even during stronger weeks
  • One-on-one time with each child matters more than it seems
  • Ordinary appointments often reveal how fast life is moving
  • The body can feel strong in the morning and uncertain by evening
