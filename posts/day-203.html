Day 203: A Runner Again

"A Sunday that quietly marked another milestone in recovery. Instead of heading to the gym, I tested my body in a more functional way with a 5 km run. The biggest victory wasn't the pace, it was finishing without pain. The rest of the day revolved around sport, family, and appreciating how far this journey has already taken me."

Pain Level 1/10
Swelling 1/10

Sunday started differently.

For the first time in a while, I skipped the gym. The boys had football practice, and instead of standing on the sidelines the whole time, I decided to run around the block.

Three laps.

Five kilometers.

I settled into a pace of around six minutes per kilometer and, to my surprise, felt good. Even better, there was no pain. The leg responded well, and for the first time I wasn't constantly thinking about every step. A few weeks ago, running outside felt awkward and uncomfortable, especially downhill. Today it felt much more natural.

It was another small milestone.

After the run, I watched the Roland Garros final. I was incredibly happy to see Alexander Zverev finally lift the trophy after so many years of setbacks and disappointments. As someone who has followed his career closely and experienced my own struggles with injuries, it was satisfying to see persistence rewarded. He deserved it.

This time we skipped the usual family lunch, but later in the afternoon we went to my in-laws' house. The atmosphere was relaxed, and I spent a good part of the afternoon playing baseball with the kids. Moments like these make me realize that my recovery is no longer measured only by gym sessions or physio exercises, but by the activities I can share with my family.

There was, unsurprisingly, more wine.

And eventually, we headed back home for a movie night.

Looking back on the day, the biggest achievement wasn't watching great tennis or spending time with family.

It was running five kilometers without pain and realizing that, little by little, my body is starting to trust itself again.

Key Takeaways

  • Running outdoors is becoming more natural and comfortable
  • Completing 5 km without pain is an important recovery milestone
  • Progress is now measured by confidence as much as strength
  • Sharing sports with my children is becoming part of my rehabilitation
  • Persistence, whether in tennis or recovery, is eventually rewarded
