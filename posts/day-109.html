Day 109: Running Returns

"A day that combined productivity, recovery, and one important physical breakthrough. The knee felt more cooperative during rehab, patellar discomfort was lower, and for the first time in almost four months running returned, awkward and tiring, but possible. What lasted only a few minutes felt much bigger than the time itself."

Pain Level 2/10
Swelling 2/10

The day started with a gym session followed by a quick fifteen-minute massage, which already gave the body a good start before the usual morning chaos began. Sending the kids to school always creates its own rhythm, but today there was one big advantage: I was working from home, so I avoided the usual stress of rushing out and sitting in traffic on the way to the office. That alone changes the tone of the day more than it should.

Before opening the laptop, I did a short mobility session, just enough to loosen the knee and prepare the body. Then I moved into a very productive stretch of work. Later I joined an online event about AI and how fast it is changing our lives. It was genuinely interesting to listen and observe how quickly everything is evolving right now, with so many shifts happening at the

same time that it almost feels difficult to absorb where things are heading.

At 17:00 I met Cleyber. The exercises already feel easier now, and one thing that stood out immediately was how much less pain I felt in the patella. That gave us confidence to push a bit more than usual. Then came something I had been waiting for without fully realizing how strange it would feel: we tried running.

The first moments were almost shocking. My brain had clearly forgotten what running feels like. After almost four months, the body knew the movement, but the sensation felt foreign, as if I was reconnecting with something distant. I ran only five minutes and was completely exhausted. It began with pain, hesitation, and fear, but little by little confidence increased, almost in parallel with the fatigue. The discomfort moved through the calves, quads, and knee, yet it never crossed into something alarming. It was simply the body remembering.

That made it a major milestone, one of those moments that carries much more emotional weight than the action itself suggests.

Afterward we went upstairs for release work because many muscles were painful and tight after the effort. Back home, I continued with PowerDot massage and ice, wanting to settle everything properly after such a new stimulus.

The day ended with friends visiting and a quiet sense that another important step had been reached, one worth acknowledging even if recovery still has a long way to go.

Key Takeaways

  • Working from home removes invisible stress that affects the body
  • Patellar pain is losing intensity during stronger exercises
  • Running returns first as discomfort, then as confidence
  • Major milestones often arrive through very small actions
  • Recovery is becoming less theoretical and more physical again
