Day 166: Long Drives and Slow Recovery

"A holiday shaped by family rhythm, interrupted rest, and long hours on the road. The body handled the day reasonably well, but extended time sitting in the car once again reminded me that travel remains one of the most difficult parts of recovery."

Pain Level 2/10
Swelling 1-2/10

It was another holiday today, which meant the kids stayed home. They woke me up early, and despite trying to recover the two hours of sleep I lost the night before, there was no chance. Too much noise, too much movement, too much life happening around me.

So I gave up on sleeping longer, got up, and had breakfast.

Juliana went to the gym, which meant I stayed with the kids. While watching the semifinals of the Madrid Open, I decided to squeeze in a quick core session at home. Nothing major, but enough to feel like I did something productive physically.

The house actually felt calmer because we only had two kids with us. Gabriel had gone the day before to stay at a friend's house outside São Paulo, and the plan was to pick him up later in the afternoon.

The drive there reminded me again how difficult long periods in the car still are for me. After around two hours sitting, my leg started complaining. Not sharp pain, but stiffness and discomfort building steadily. It's interesting how movement keeps getting easier, but inactivity still creates problems.

Once we arrived, though, the whole mood changed. We had a great time at our friends' house and stayed there until around 20:00. Good conversations, relaxed atmosphere, kids enjoying themselves. Fortunately, the drive back was shorter, around one hour and twenty minutes, which made a big difference.

Back home, we put the kids to sleep and finally got some quiet television time together with Juliana.

It ended up being a long day, but one that slowly softened toward the end.

Key Takeaways

  • Long drives remain physically demanding during recovery
  • Movement feels easier than prolonged inactivity
  • Even short home sessions help maintain consistency
  • Family rhythm often defines the pace of recovery days
  • Quiet endings help balance exhausting days
