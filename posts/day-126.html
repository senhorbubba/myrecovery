Day 126: Running on Empty

"A day driven by exhaustion, poor sleep, and the physical cost of the previous days. Everything felt slower and heavier, both mentally and physically. The focus shifted to getting through the day rather than performing in it, with travel adding another layer of discomfort."

Pain Level 2/10
Swelling 2/10

I slept only three hours and woke up with a heavy hangover. One of those mornings where everything feels off from the start. Walking felt uncoordinated, almost like moving backwards, and my thinking was slow, delayed, as if my mind needed extra time to process even simple things.

I forced myself to eat something healthier and focused on drinking more water, knowing that there was no shortcut out of this state. At the same time, I had to start packing, since we were leaving that night. It was not the kind of day where anything feels smooth. Everything required effort.

We went out for a last lunch with Yang, who was also leaving, heading to Vegas. It was a good moment to close that part of the trip, even if the energy was not there to fully enjoy it. After that, a bit of last-minute shopping and a quick drink with Bartek and his friends and family. Even that felt like pushing more than the body wanted, but it was part of the flow of the day.

The flight itself was probably the hardest part. Tight space, very tired, unable to get comfortable. At some point, the service cart kept hitting my knee repeatedly, which added another layer of frustration. Luckily, it was the other knee, not the one in recovery, but still enough to make the whole experience uncomfortable.

There was no real rhythm to the day, just moving from one thing to another, managing energy, and getting through it.

Some days are not about progress. They are about endurance.

Key Takeaways

  • Lack of sleep affects everything, body and mind
  • Recovery days still exist even in the middle of travel
  • Not every day needs to be productive to be valid
  • Sometimes the goal is simply to get through the day
  • Awareness of limits becomes clearer through fatigue
