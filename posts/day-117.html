Day 117: Moving Again

"A day built around movement in every sense: family rhythm, work pressure, training progression, and travel. The body worked hard in the morning, the mind stayed sharp through the day, and by the time the journey began there was a clear sense that this trip already felt different from the last one. Recovery is no longer defined by restriction, but by how naturally life starts fitting around it again."

Pain Level 1/10
Swelling 2/10

It was a travel day, and like most days, it began with the usual morning routine with the kids, which always sets the emotional tone before anything else starts. Once they were out, I sat down at the desk and immediately felt the pressure of how much still needed to be done before leaving. At least my mind was clear today, and that made efficiency possible. There are days when everything feels heavy before it even starts, but today was not one of them.

Before diving fully into work, I quickly went to see Taka. This session felt harder than the last one. We introduced some new tennis-specific exercises and worked the core in a more demanding way. The whole session felt more dynamic, more athletic, and somehow more connected to where I want to return. We pushed hard, and by the end I was very tired, but also with that satisfying feeling that progress is becoming visible not only in strength, but in the type of movement I can now handle. The exercises are changing, becoming less repetitive and more interesting, which itself feels like a sign of moving into a different phase.

Back home, straight into work again. Quick lunch, packing, then more work before the kids returned. When they got back, I consciously gave them my full attention for those last two hours before leaving. I have noticed something important recently: I feel much less stress when I stop trying to divide myself. If I stop working and am simply with them, fully present, everything becomes lighter. There is a calm in that full attention that I did not appreciate enough before.

In the evening I left for the airport. Traffic was heavy, but I still managed to finish a few things in the car, which somehow made the journey feel useful instead of wasted. At the airport I met a friend and we travelled together. There was probably too much wine to start the trip, but at least I ate lightly, which helped.

Boarding the plane brought back memories of my last trip. The comparison came naturally. This time there were no crutches, no children, no physical logistics to calculate every minute. That alone changed the entire experience. Small things that used to feel complicated now simply passed without thought.

Once seated, I put on my compression socks and decided to sleep. I woke up a few times during the flight, each time moving the knee, pumping the legs, and drinking water before falling asleep again.

The knee felt good throughout. Stable, quiet, reliable.

Key Takeaways

  • Training is becoming more specific and closer to sport
  • Dynamic exercises make progress feel more real
  • Presence reduces stress more than multitasking ever does
  • Travel feels very different when recovery no longer dominates logistics
  • Stability is one of the most reassuring signs of progress
