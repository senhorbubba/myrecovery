Day 115: A Different Perspective on Timing

"The day began with a strong session that brought useful perspective. Physically, the body responded well, but the most important takeaway was not about what I can do, but about what still needs patience. Recovery continues to move forward, yet today was a reminder that progress is not only measured by ability, but by respecting timing."

Pain Level 2/10
Swelling 2/10

Before work I went to see Taka. I always like these sessions because they give me a slightly different perspective on where I really am in the recovery process. Even when the exercises are similar to things I already do, the way he observes movement and explains progression often shifts how I understand the stage I am in.

Today we pushed hard. The exercises were demanding, not because of pain, but because I could really feel the muscles working deeply, especially where strength still needs to come back. That kind of fatigue feels positive because it shows clearly where the body still lacks confidence and endurance.

One thing he emphasized was that there is no point in rushing recovery. We spoke about running, and his view was clear: for me it still seems early. He explained that starting to run is not only about feeling capable for a few minutes. There are criteria that should be respected: flexion, extension, range of movement, pain level, and strength. Then he added one more factor that many people forget, time itself. According to him, even if the body seems willing, running should only begin around four months after surgery, and only when the other conditions are also in place.

In my case, pain is still present in some movements and strength still needs improvement, which means there is still work to do before this becomes natural and sustainable. Hearing that did not frustrate me. In a way, it actually made things clearer. Sometimes the body gives enthusiasm before it gives full readiness, and maybe that is exactly when caution matters most.

We finished the session with massage, painful in parts as usual, followed by mobility exercises. By the end I felt noticeably better, lighter, with the knee moving more freely.

I went straight to work after that. Even though the day again involved long periods sitting, I felt better than usual, almost as if the morning session had prepared the body for the rest of the day. There is clearly something about training early that changes everything afterward.

The rest of the day I kept easy. No additional training, no stretching, just allowing the body to absorb the work done in the morning.

Key Takeaways

  • Progress also means hearing when not to accelerate
  • Running is not only about ability, but also about readiness
  • Time remains an essential part of recovery
  • Morning sessions improve how the body feels through the day
  • Muscle fatigue without pain is a good signal of honest work
