Day 199: A Reality Check

"A day that reminded me how different controlled rehabilitation is from real-life movement. Running on roads with hills and uneven surfaces exposed weaknesses that the gym cannot simulate. It was a valuable test, not because it went perfectly, but because it showed exactly where I still need to improve."

Pain Level 2/10
Swelling 1-2/10

I worked from home today. There was simply too much to do, and skipping the commute gave me a few extra productive hours.

In the afternoon, I had to pick up Kai from his tennis class. Normally, one of his friends gives him a ride back home, but since his friend wasn't there today, there was nobody to bring him back.

So I decided to turn it into an experiment.

I would run there.

It's not far, around 2.5 kilometers, but this was very different from running on a treadmill. The moment I started, I realized how many variables the outside world adds. The road goes uphill, downhill, changes direction, changes surface.

And downhill was by far the hardest.

Every step required control, and every impact reminded me that my knee is still learning how to absorb force naturally again. It wasn't impossible, but it certainly wasn't easy. There was discomfort, effort, and moments where I questioned whether this had been a good idea.

But I made it.

I arrived just in time to watch the end of the match, where Berrettini was forced to retire because of a wrist injury. It was sad to see. He had worked so hard to get his tennis back to the level he showed a few years ago, and injuries continue to interrupt his momentum.

After Kai's practice, he wanted to keep playing. The courts were empty, so we stayed another thirty or forty minutes hitting balls together.

I couldn't say no.

By the time we finished, my knee was tired. Not injured, just tired.

Today was a good experience because it reminded me that rehabilitation happens in stages. The gym makes me stronger. Physio makes me more stable. But real life still exposes weaknesses that machines cannot replicate.

And that's okay.

I'd rather discover those limitations now than rush into thinking I'm fully back.

There is still a long way to go.

But there is also no doubt anymore that I'm getting there.

Key Takeaways

  • Real-world running is far more demanding than treadmill running
  • Downhill movement remains one of the biggest challenges for the knee
  • Strength gained in the gym still needs to translate into functional movement
  • Playing with my kids is becoming part of my rehabilitation journey
  • Progress is measured not by perfection but by discovering the next challenge
