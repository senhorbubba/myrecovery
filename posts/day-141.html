Day 141: Closer, But Not There Yet

"A day that highlighted steady physical progress while also exposing the gap that still exists to full return. The knee feels better in everyday life, and strength is improving, but the feeling of being truly ready for sport is not there yet. Progress is real, but patience is still required."

Pain Level 1/10
Swelling 1/10

My knee is getting better. I can feel it clearly now. I wake up without pain, I get out of bed without that shaking in the leg that used to be there. It has been 140 days already. When I look back, there is a lot of improvement, but at the same time I can feel that I am still far from real sport.

People say that around this time you start to feel ready to go back, but I don’t feel that yet. Physically maybe I could try more, but mentally and in terms of confidence, I am not there.

The morning was simple. Breakfast with the kids, quick routine, then to the office. A lot of sitting again. I had to leave earlier than usual to meet Cleyber at 17:00.

The sessions are becoming a routine now. The exercises are repetitive and honestly quite boring, even the new ones that simulate movement on the court. But having someone there makes a big difference. Without that push, it would be easy to skip or reduce the intensity.

Today we worked hard. We started running again and added side steps with bands. These movements start to resemble tennis more and more. For a moment, it gives you the feeling of being an athlete again, but it is still far from what it feels like on the court.

We pushed hard, and I could feel more power in my legs and less pain during the exercises. That combination makes me happy. It shows that something is clearly moving in the right direction.

Back home, I felt tired. Not exhausted, but that kind of fatigue that comes from a good session.

I ended the day with a book and went to bed early.

Closer, but not there yet.

Key Takeaways

  • Daily improvements are becoming more noticeable in simple movements
  • Strength is increasing while pain continues to decrease
  • Sport-specific exercises highlight the gap to full return
  • Consistency remains the most important factor in progress
  • Patience is still required despite visible improvement
