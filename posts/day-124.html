Day 124: Between Return and Hesitation

"A day split between two sports environments, golf in the morning and tennis in the evening, both highlighting how close and yet how far full return still feels. Physically present, but mentally still cautious, especially in movements that require trust. The day carried strong social energy, but also a quiet internal tension around performance and confidence."

Pain Level 2/10
Swelling 2/10

I woke up at 6:00 to drive to Trump Doral Golf Course. The plan was not to play, just to spend time with friends and be around the game again. Still, I could not resist trying a few approach shots and putting. The putting felt decent, controlled and familiar, but the approach shots told a different story. Five months away is noticeable. I could not control the ball the way I used to, and more than that, there was a constant thought in the back of my mind that I might hurt myself.

That feeling is uncomfortable. It is not physical pain, but something deeper, a hesitation that sits between intention and execution. I can already tell that this mental part will take time, probably longer than the physical recovery itself.

We stayed there until late morning, then grabbed a quick American-style lunch, a classic burger, before heading back to pick up our wives. Once home, there was barely time to reset. A quick change, a bit of rest, and then we were on the move again, heading back to the tournament.

We arrived around 17:00 and met my good friend Yang, who had just arrived from London. It was great to catch up. These kinds of meetings always carry a different energy, mixing past and present in a way that feels natural. The evening passed quickly, watching more great tennis and just enjoying being there, surrounded by the sport again.

By the time we got back home, the day had fully caught up with me. A few last drinks, then ice and sleep.

A long day, full in every sense, but with that quiet reminder that coming back is not only about the body being ready, it is also about trusting it again.

Key Takeaways

  • Being physically able does not immediately remove mental hesitation
  • Time away from sport shows clearly in precision and control
  • Fear of re-injury is subtle but present in technical movements
  • Returning to sport requires rebuilding trust, not just strength
  • Social energy can carry the day even when the body feels the load
