Day 180: Feeling Like an Athlete Again

"A milestone kind of Friday where tennis once again became part of normal life. The body is still adapting and limitations are still present, but the feeling of movement, fatigue, and progression brought back a sense of identity that had been missing for months. At the same time, the social rebound from recent low moods continues pulling me toward excess again."

Pain Level 1-2/10
Swelling 1/10

It's Friday again, tennis day.

I followed the same routine as last week: biking twenty minutes to the club, hitting standing tennis, then biking twenty minutes back home. On the ride back, I even started beating some of my personal times. Next goal is going below twenty minutes. Funny how quickly the competitive mindset returns once movement starts feeling natural again.

Before playing, I also adjusted my racket and string setup to protect my arm more. Since I still cannot move properly with my legs and I'm playing mostly static tennis, there is much more load going into the arm and shoulder. I definitely don't want my golfer's elbow to come back, or even worse, develop a shoulder injury while recovering from the knee.

I switched to a hybrid setup and slightly bigger head rackets. The difference was immediate. Balls feel easier to hit, more forgiving, and most importantly I don't feel the wrist discomfort anymore.

Small adjustments, but they matter.

By the time I biked back home, my legs were tired in the best possible way. I missed that feeling so much. The kind of tiredness that comes from sport, movement, and effort instead of rehab alone.

Back home, I worked for a while and later went out again for a happy hour with another friend. Juliana already started making jokes about how suddenly I became social again after weeks of lower mood and isolation.

And honestly, she's right.

I can clearly feel my mood improving lately. The problem is that together with the social energy, the alcohol also came back stronger than it probably should.

I ended up drinking a lot again.

Got home around midnight and went straight to sleep.

Still, despite the excess, today felt important.

For a few hours, I stopped feeling like someone recovering from surgery and started feeling like an athlete again.

Key Takeaways

  • Tennis is becoming part of normal life again
  • Equipment adjustments can protect the body during recovery
  • Athletic fatigue feels emotionally different from rehab fatigue
  • Social recovery can sometimes drift into excess
  • The identity of being an athlete is slowly returning
