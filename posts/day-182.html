Day 182: Listening to the Body

"A slow recovery day after a busy and social weekend. The body clearly demanded rest, and for once I listened. Fatigue, dehydration, and a slightly fuller knee served as reminders that progress does not eliminate the need for recovery. The day became less about pushing forward and more about recharging for the week ahead."

Pain Level 2/10
Swelling 2/10

The morning was difficult.

I woke up alone as Juliana had already left for practice with her band. I practically crawled myself out of bed. Recovery was nowhere to be found today. My vitals were off, I felt dehydrated, tired, and generally drained after the last few days.

I turned on the final of the Rome Open and watched Jannik Sinner play Casper Ruud. It was great to see Ruud back competing at such a high level, but it was even more impressive to watch Sinner continue his rise. He seems to be building something special.

Afterward, I did some yoga and mobility work. The knee was still making its usual funny noises, but today it also felt a little fuller than normal. Not dramatically swollen, just enough to notice.

Naturally, my mind started asking questions.

Was it the tennis session on Friday?

Was it all the dancing and standing on Saturday?

Or maybe just the accumulation of everything together?

In the end, I decided not to overanalyze it and instead focused on recovery.

The rest of the day was intentionally easy. The kids came back, and we spent time watching a movie, playing games, and doing very little else. Exactly what I needed.

In the evening, I used the massage gun on my legs and finished with some PowerDot recovery work. Nothing fancy, just helping the body absorb the weekend and prepare for the week ahead.

Tomorrow a new week starts.

And on Wednesday I finally see Dr. Sergio again.

I'm genuinely excited to hear what he thinks about the progress and what the next steps will be.

Key Takeaways

  • Recovery becomes more important as activity levels increase
  • Not every ache or swelling requires immediate concern
  • Listening to the body can be more valuable than pushing through fatigue
  • Family downtime can be a powerful form of recovery
  • The next phase of rehabilitation feels close, and that brings excitement as well as curiosity
