Day 119: Slow Pace, Full Day

"The day began with sunlight, training, and a good sense of structure, then slowly shifted into a long Sunday built around friends, conversation, food, and an easy social rhythm. Energy stayed lower than usual after the previous days, but movement in the morning helped create balance and reduced some of the guilt that often appears when the day becomes indulgent."

Pain Level 1/10
Swelling 2/10

I woke up early with the sun directly on my face, one of those mornings where the light immediately tells you the day has already started whether you want it or not. After a quick breakfast I went to the gym. The session focused on chest, triceps, and legs, and for the first time I consciously incorporated a few of the exercises Taka introduced recently. They really make a difference. The tennis-specific movements change the feeling of the session completely, making it more dynamic and mentally more engaging than a standard routine. There is something motivating about movements that clearly connect to where I want to return rather than simply building strength in isolation.

Back home I slowed down. Rest, shower, some food, then a stop at Whole Foods for groceries and a few extra things. Everything felt slower today. My thinking was slower, my movements slower, almost as if the previous days were still sitting in the body and asking for a lower rhythm.

Later we decided to go to Wynwood, to El Tropical, for an easy Sunday with friends. The plan naturally became beer, conversation, and tennis on screen, with the Indian Wells final playing in the background. Medvedev once again could not find a way past Sinner, and watching that level of control from Sinner makes it easier to understand why he currently feels almost impossible to break.

The day kept unfolding without much structure after that. More drinks, very good barbecue, then a kind of spontaneous pub crawl where one place naturally led to another. It became one of those Sundays that stretches much further than expected without anyone really deciding it.

The gym session in the morning helped psychologically because at least I knew I had already done something good for the body before the day drifted into indulgence. Still, by the time I came back home I felt heavy, physically and mentally, the kind of heaviness that comes from mixing too much standing, food, and alcohol over many hours.

Before sleep I did a quick ice session, enough to close the day properly.

A funny day, relaxed, social, and somehow very fast despite the slower pace inside me.

Key Takeaways

  • Tennis-specific exercises make training more motivating
  • The body sometimes asks for slower rhythm even when the day keeps moving
  • Morning discipline changes how indulgence feels later
  • Long social days still accumulate heaviness by evening
  • Some weekends pass faster than expected
